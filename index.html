02 · Design

Six parts, fully dimensioned

  • All parts sized around the DC130 motor

03 · Simulation

Blade aerodynamics in ANSYS Fluent

  • 3D blade at 8.9 m/s: rotor torque 6.79 mN·m
  • 2D sweep 0–45°: lift–drag gap widest near 12°

  3. Test 2: fixed

    Loosened nacelle, removed foam → 10 mA at 35 mV; power 9% above cohort average, 1st place.

Sheet 03 · Hatton Lab, U of T · Sep 2024 – May 2025

Microfluidic Wound-Healing Device & Bioink

Device iteration and collagen bioink testing.

My contribution

Problem solving
Traced poor cell growth to high-passage cells and expired medium; viability +10%.
Design
Redesigned channel geometry and count; drug-distribution uniformity +30%.
Testing

Result

Role
Research Assistant (incl. thesis)

Methods

Result

01 · Context

A dressing that delivers skin cells

  • Burn dressing that delivers cells and drugs evenly across large wounds (up to 200 cm²)
  • My part: device testing and optimization, bioink testing

02 · Device iteration

Test, optimize, retest

  • Base design (M. Karimi, Prof. Hatton): H-tree channels sized by Murray's law, 8–128 ports
  • My part: iterative testing. Varied channel geometry and port count while holding dye and syringe-pump flow rate (20 mL/min) constant; measured outlet distribution by confocal fluorescence and compared each round against the original design
  • Result: drug-distribution uniformity +30% vs. the original design

03 · Experiment

2D flask vs 3D collagen gel

  • HDFa on 1- and 2-layer 6% collagen gels
  • 2D flasks as controls
  • Live/dead staining on Day 9

04 · Culture log

Cells attach, then spread

  • 24 h: dead cells float as debris; rinsing leaves live cells attached
  • Day 3: only 20–40% confluency (target 80%), the first sign of a problem
  • Day 5: cells elongate and flatten as they spread

05 · Problem solving

Two failures, two causes found

  1. Cells barely grew

    P8 cells + expired medium → switched to P3–P5, in-date reagents.

  2. 1-layer gel washed away

    Thin gel delaminates under cell tension → 2-layer minimum.

  3. Follow-up runs

    Penetration depth +13%.

Smart Hydrogel for Osteoarthritis

Releases IL-1Ra on inflammation and on load.

Design
One depot: HA-Tyr gel, catechol adhesion, two responsive PLGA carriers.
Problem solving
A planned fallback for each technical risk.
Result
Complete conceptual design and 3-stage test plan.
Role
Co-designer with Jojo Peng
Type
Conceptual design
Methods
Material selection · targets · test planning
Result
Design + 3-stage test plan

01 · Design

Four parts, four jobs

ComponentJobTarget
HA-tyramine gelCartilage-like matrix200–400 kPa · gels in 10–60 s
Catechol groupsStick to wet cartilage5–10 mol%
ROS-cleavable PLGASteady dose in inflammationDegrades at 5–10× ROS
Mechano-responsive PLGABurst dose on overloadRuptures at 3–5 MPa

02 · Validation

Three stages

  1. Materials

    Rheology, SEM, BCA loading, 30,000 load cycles.

  2. Cells + cartilage

    OA chondrocytes: viability >80%, qPCR/ELISA, adhesion pull-out.

  3. Mouse model

    16-week DMM vs free IL-1Ra: OARSI score, micro-CT.

03 · Risks

Planned fallbacks

  1. Fast degradation

    Switch to PEG or PLGA/PEG carriers.

  2. Poor penetration

    250–300 nm vesicles or surface charge.

  3. Weak suppression

    More IL-1Ra or add MMP-13 inhibitor.

Sheet 06 · Individual project · Sep 2023 – Jan 2024

Guitar String Deformation, CAD + FEA

Why strings deform: SolidWorks model, ANSYS study.

3D model · from my SolidWorks file

Loading 3D model…

My contribution

What
Modeled a 988 mm guitar in SolidWorks; compared brass and nylon-6 strings in ANSYS.
Problem solving
Simplified to a 0.5 mm-radius string on a fixed body; applied a 20 N fret press and 20 N pluck from measured finger forces.
Result
At similar peak stress, nylon deformed ~90× more than brass: flexible vs stiff. Upgrade path: SMAT brass + coating.
Role
Individual project
Tools
SolidWorks 2022 · ANSYS 2023 R2
Materials
Brass C37700 · nylon 6 · oak · steel
Result
Nylon ~90× more deformation than brass

01 · FEA

Same load, two string materials

  • Full guitar model built first; string study run on a simplified body
  • Body fixed; string 0.5 mm radius; mesh 10 mm body, 5 mm string
  • Loads: 20 N fret press (Y) + 20 N pluck (Z)

02 · Results

Nylon bends, brass resists

  • Peak equivalent stress: nearly the same for both
  • Total deformation and elastic strain: nylon ~90× brass
  • Nylon absorbs more energy; brass holds shape (stiffer)

03 · Materials

Nylon 6 vs brass

  • Pressing a fret stretches the string L₀ → Lᵢ
  • Higher tension, thinner section, pitch shift
  • Repeated presses → fatigue
PropertyNylon 6Brass
Young's modulus1.3–4.2 GPa97 GPa
Yield strength40–100 MPa138 MPa
Tensile strength50–90 MPa124–310 MPa
Density1.1 g/cm³8.5 g/cm³

04 · Upgrade path

Longer life under cyclic load

  • SMAT: gradient grains raise brass yield strength
  • Coating: blocks corrosion, the top reason strings are replaced
  • MPI selection: light, slender, high stored elastic energy
